Nepal Flood Relief · Trishuli Valley · August 2026

The valley where our work began is underwater.

On August 26, a glacial collapse in Tibet sent a wall of water and debris down the Bhotekoshi and the Trishuli, through Rasuwa, Nuwakot, and six more districts. More than 1,000 people are confirmed dead, at least 51 of them in Nuwakot, and nearly 4,000 are still missing. These are the villages our coffee comes from: home of Nuwa Estate, our partner on this land, and the farming families Nuwa has known by name since 2007. They are the people we work with and support.

Our partners have worked in this valley for decades, and our commitment to it will outlast the cameras. In October, we will be on the ground ourselves, documenting the mountain regions of Nepal: the people, the villages, and the rebuilding.

How to stand with the valley
Why Give Here

This valley is personal.

Every disaster appeal asks for your trust. Ours rests on partnerships measured in decades, not news cycles.

Since 2007, Nuwa Estate has anchored this district: the Tenzing family farm, the schools its harvests help fund, the families whose livelihoods keep the forest standing. We are the estate’s long-term US partner, our foundation exists to serve mountain communities like these, and the flood came down their river.

The Nepal Flood Recovery Fund does one integrated work: emergency and rebuilding grants through our long-term partners on the ground, organizations we have worked beside for decades that are already distributing relief in the affected districts, and our October field mission, when we extend our own trip to document the mountain regions, tell the stories of the Nepali people, and work alongside our partners on the ground. This is not a disaster pop-up. We are invested in this valley for the long term, and the flood has made one thing plain: these communities need long-term solutions, not just emergency kits.

How the money is handled: Planet 5 covers the Foundation's operating costs, and we fund our own base travel personally. The fund pays only for relief and rebuilding grants on the ground and the direct field costs of the documentation mission, under a budget approved by the Foundation's independent directors. The majority of the fund goes directly to grants in the valley, and every dollar is publicly accounted for.

Our Connection

Our coffee comes from Nuwakot.

Nuwa Estate sits at 1,400 metres in Nuwakot, one of the districts the flood passed through. The farm belongs to the family of Tenzing Norgay, and we are its long-term US partner, bringing its coffee to America exclusively.

Its sales already help fund schools in the same district. Our commitment is to the commerce, the coffee, the schools, and the people of this region for the long run. The flood does not change that commitment; it deepens it.

Our Philosophy

Commerce, not charity.

A donation is spent once. A US market for Nuwakot coffee pays every harvest, for decades, and every bag that leaves the district is income earned rather than aid received.

We are asking for long-term partners in that recovery, not a one-time gesture. Emergency relief matters this month; a working economy is what rebuilds a valley for good. That is why this fund pairs rebuilding grants with our October field mission, and why the strongest thing you can do after giving is simple: drink the valley's coffee.
