The Trishuli valley needs help.

Why Give Here

This valley is personal.

Our connection to this valley is measured in decades, not news cycles.

Mountain Preservation Foundation is a nonprofit that protects mountain landscapes by investing in the people who live in them. We were created to stand behind local partners who have worked in this valley for nearly two decades, and the Nepal Flood Recovery Fund is our first program. More about the Foundation →

Since 2007, Nuwa Estate has been part of the fabric of this place: the Tenzing family farm, the schools and clinics its harvests help support, and the farming families whose livelihoods are rooted here. Our relationship with Nuwa brought us to this valley long before the flood.

We intend to be there long after the headlines have moved on, helping restore livelihoods, strengthen local economies and support the communities that call this place home for the long term.

But today, the need is here.

Nepal Flood Recovery Fund

Our response begins with the people already there.

Through long-standing partners on the ground, the Nepal Flood Recovery Fund supports immediate relief and rebuilding in affected communities, putting resources directly into the hands of organizations and people who know these villages, know these families, and understand what is needed now.

Where Your Gift Goes

Planet 5 has committed a portion of Guardian Program revenue to help support the Foundation’s operating costs, and our team funds its base travel personally.

Donations to the Nepal Flood Recovery Fund go to our partners on the ground for direct short and long term assistance in the affected communities.

The goal is simple: keep as much of your gift as possible where it belongs, in the valley, helping people recover and rebuild.

October

In October, we will be there ourselves.

Our team will be in Nepal alongside Nuwa Estate, the Nepal Green Tara Foundation and other local partners. We’ll visit the farm, fly over the flood-affected areas, meet with families and partners, and listen to what these communities need next.

We’ll bring home the stories of the people, the photographs, the progress and the work still ahead.
