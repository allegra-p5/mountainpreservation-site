Why Mountains

Mountains hold more than peaks.

They hold the headwaters that supply much of the world’s fresh water. Forests that shelter extraordinary biodiversity. Cultures shaped over generations. And communities whose lives are inseparable from the landscapes around them.

Yet mountain regions remain among the world’s most vulnerable, and least supported, ecosystems.

We believe protecting them requires looking at the whole mountain. The land. The water. The wildlife. And the people who call it home.

Our Mission

To preserve the world’s great mountain ranges and to strengthen the communities who call them home. We fund the permanent protection of high-mountain forests, watersheds, and wildlife corridors. We build and equip schools and health clinics in remote mountain villages, and we invest in safe, dignified livelihoods that keep forests standing and families together.

Our Vision

A world where every great mountain range is preserved by the people who live beneath it; where standing forests, thriving villages, open wildlife corridors, and safe childhoods are one and the same achievement.

The Origin

It began with one family.

In 2007, while Tashi Wangchuk Tenzing, grandson of Tenzing Norgay, was summiting Everest for the third time, his wife Bandi Nima Sherpa Tenzing stood above the terraced hills of Nuwakot district and saw something beyond the landscape: a community where human trafficking and emigration were draining families from the mountains.

She decided to change the economics. She believed the mountains could only be protected if families could prosper without leaving them. The Tenzings planted coffee. From that decision grew a farm that supports hundreds of families, and a nonprofit that builds schools, equips clinics, sponsors children, and runs anti-trafficking programs across the Himalaya.

The coffee is the product. The protection is the purpose. The Foundation exists to carry that purpose to every great range.

Our Work

One mountain. One system.

Our work brings together five things that are too often addressed separately.

Land

Landscape protection

Protect forests, watersheds, wildlife habitat and the landscapes that connect them: high-mountain forests that are home to red pandas, Himalayan black bears, and hundreds of bird species.

Livelihoods

Durable local economies

Income rooted in a standing forest: coffee, forest products and eco-tourism that give young people a reason to stay.

Education

Schools and teachers

Building, equipping, and sustaining schools in remote mountain villages, with teacher support for children who would otherwise have no access.

Health

Clinics and care

Rural health clinics serving mountain populations far from the nearest hospital, where geography makes access difficult.

People

Children and families

Anti-trafficking programs, child sponsorship, and women’s leadership through coffee cooperatives, so families can build a future at home.

Because when a village can thrive, families can stay. When families can stay, local knowledge remains. And when communities have a stake in protecting the land around them, preservation has a chance to last.

Our Partners

This work didn’t begin with us. And that’s important.

Nuwa Estate, the Nepal Green Tara Foundation and local partners were building schools, supporting clinics and creating livelihoods in Nepal long before the foundation existed.

Mountain Preservation Foundation was created to stand behind that work, help it grow, and build a preservation model capable of reaching mountain communities far beyond Nepal.

Beyond Nepal

Nepal is where the work begins. It is not where it ends.

The challenges facing mountain communities are global. From the Himalaya to Africa, the Andes, the Rockies and other great ranges, environmental pressure and economic pressure are often part of the same story.

We are building an approach designed to travel, but never to be imposed. Every place is different. Every community knows its mountain better than we ever will. So wherever we go, the principle remains the same:

Work locally. Build long relationships. Protect the mountain.

What follows is a roadmap of planned phases, not commitments made, and it will change as we learn.

  • Kenya: preservation partnerships with Maasai communities at Mt Kenya and Amboseli, planned for 2027.
  • Bolivia: highland cooperatives in the Yungas, planned for 2027.
  • Ethiopia: Yirgacheffe and Sidama, in the pipeline.
  • Patagonia: named from the start.
How We Are Funded

A different way to fund preservation.

Mountain Preservation Foundation was designed so that philanthropy can remain focused on the work.

Planet 5 has committed a portion of revenue from its Guardian Program to help support the Foundation’s operating costs. That commitment is made under a written agreement, and our commercial and philanthropic activities remain separate, with independent governance and accounting. The Guardian Program is a Planet 5 product; you can read about it on planet5media.com.

Donors to the Foundation are giving to the Foundation alone. Gifts carry no goods, services, or membership benefits. Designated gifts, such as the Nepal Flood Recovery Fund, are reserved for the designated program.

The purpose is simple: keep more philanthropic capital where it belongs, in the mountains.

Accountability

We measure what we promise.

Good intentions aren’t enough. We are building our work around clear goals and measurable outcomes.

GovernanceOur commercial and philanthropic activities are kept separate, with written agreements and separate books.
TransparencyOur IRS filings are public records, and we will make them available on request.
Donor intentDesignated gifts are applied to the designated program. If a program becomes fully funded, we apply the balance to the closest related need and let you know.

The Foundation was incorporated in Wyoming on August 24, 2026. Our first program is the Nepal Flood Recovery Fund. As grants are made, we intend to share what they funded and what changed because of them: habitat protected, livelihoods supported, and schools and health programs strengthened.
