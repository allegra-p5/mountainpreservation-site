This valley is personal.

On August 26, flash floods tore through communities along the Trishuli River in Rasuwa and Nuwakot, the valley where our work began. Homes, farmland, roads and critical infrastructure were damaged or destroyed.

For MPF, this isn’t a distant disaster. Our partners are there. The farming families are there. The schools and communities we know are there.

The Nepal Flood Recovery Fund is supporting immediate relief and rebuilding through people who were in the valley long before the flood and will remain long after it. In October, our team will be there ourselves: to listen, document the recovery, understand what is needed next and continue the work for the long term.

We knew this valley before the flood. We will be here for what comes after.

Why Mountains

Mountains hold more than peaks.

They hold the headwaters that supply much of the world’s fresh water. Forests that shelter extraordinary biodiversity. Cultures shaped over generations. And communities whose lives are inseparable from the landscapes around them.

Yet mountain regions remain among the world’s most vulnerable, and least supported, ecosystems.

We believe protecting them requires looking at the whole mountain. The land. The water. The wildlife. And the people who call it home.

The Origin

It began in one Himalayan valley.

In 2007, Bandi Nima Sherpa Tenzing looked across the terraced hills of Nepal’s Nuwakot district and saw a community under pressure. Families were leaving. Opportunity was disappearing. Forests were vulnerable. Children faced risks that reached far beyond the mountains.

She understood something fundamental: you cannot protect a place if the people who live there cannot afford to stay.

The Tenzing family planted coffee. What began as a farm became a network of growers and livelihoods. Coffee helped support schools, clinics and community programs. Local partners built relationships that have endured for nearly two decades.

Mountain Preservation Foundation grew from that same idea: preservation begins with people.

Our Work

One mountain. One system.

We don’t separate environmental preservation from the lives being lived within it. Our work brings together five things that too often are addressed separately.

Land

Landscape protection

Protect forests, watersheds, wildlife habitat and the landscapes that connect them.

Livelihoods

Durable local economies

Create local economies that make a standing forest more valuable than a cleared one.

Education

Schools and teachers

Support schools, teachers and access to education in remote mountain communities.

Health

Clinics and care

Strengthen clinics and basic healthcare where geography makes access difficult.

People

Children and families

Support programs that protect vulnerable children and families and strengthen the communities themselves.

Because when a village can thrive, families can stay. When families can stay, local knowledge remains. And when communities have a stake in protecting the land around them, preservation has a chance to last.

Our Partners

This work didn’t begin with us. And that’s important.

For nearly two decades, Nuwa Estate, the Nepal Green Tara Foundation and local partners have been doing this work in Nepal: building schools, supporting clinics, creating livelihoods and strengthening communities.

MPF was created to stand behind that work, help it grow, and build a preservation model capable of reaching mountain communities far beyond Nepal.

We begin with relationships. We listen locally. We protect what makes a place distinct. And we stay long enough to make a difference.

Beyond Nepal

Nepal is where the work begins. It is not where it ends.

The challenges facing mountain communities are global. From the Himalaya to Africa, the Andes, the Rockies and other great ranges, environmental pressure and economic pressure are often part of the same story.

MPF is building an approach designed to travel, but never to be imposed. Every place is different. Every community knows its mountain better than we ever will. So wherever we go, the principle remains the same:

Work locally. Build long relationships. Strengthen communities. Protect the mountain.

How We Are Funded

A different way to fund preservation.

Mountain Preservation Foundation was designed so that philanthropy can remain focused on the work.

Planet 5 has committed a portion of Guardian Program revenue to help support the Foundation’s operating structure. Our commercial and philanthropic activities remain separate, with independent governance and accounting.

The purpose is simple: keep more philanthropic capital where it belongs, in the mountains.

Accountability

We measure what we promise.

Good intentions aren’t enough. Our work is being built around transparent goals, independent oversight and measurable outcomes: from acres of habitat protected and livelihoods supported to schools, health programs and community initiatives.

As MPF grows, our reporting will grow with it. We intend to show not only where money went, but what changed because it was there.
